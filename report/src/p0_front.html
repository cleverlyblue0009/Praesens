Abstract

Remote identity verification — video KYC, online examinations, telehealth consultations and executive video calls — now trusts a webcam to prove that a live human is present. That trust has been broken by real-time deepfakes and, more dangerously, by video-injection attacks that bypass the camera entirely through virtual-camera drivers. Because an injected stream never passes in front of the physical sensor, conventional passive presentation-attack detectors, which inspect texture or learned visual artefacts, are blind to it, and every improvement in generative models erodes them further.

This project presents PRAESENS (Latin, “being present”), a software-only liveness framework that turns ordinary laptop hardware into an active physical challenge–response system. For every session a fresh cryptographically-seeded maximum-length (m-)sequence is synthesised and broadcast through three physically independent channels: (i) the optical lane modulates the screen border so that the light reflected by the user’s skin carries the code, which is recovered from MediaPipe facial regions by detrending, lag-searched normalised cross-correlation and in-band SNR estimation; a spatially-differential extension drives left, right and top border zones with near-orthogonal shifted codes so that each facial region must answer its own zone; (ii) the typing lane verifies that a freshly generated phrase is typed with human keystroke timing while storing no characters; and (iii) the acoustic lane BPSK-modulates the same sequence onto a 4 kHz carrier and detects its room round-trip with a drift-tolerant segmented differential matched filter. A lag-plausibility gate and an explicit binary adjudicator fuse the lanes into an explainable ACCEPT / RE-CHALLENGE / REJECT verdict.

The system was implemented in about 11,700 lines of Python with 148 automated test cases and evaluated on 73 real sessions captured on commodity hardware. On a cross-session injection proxy of 3,448 trials, the optical lane alone achieves an AUC of 0.938 and an EER of 10.2 %; bona fide sessions score a median of 0.73 against 0.055 for mismatched challenges, and the emitter-off control collapses to 0.009, proving that the score is caused by the emitted light and not by the presence of a face. All eight real virtual-camera injection sessions scored below the 0.30 pass threshold (maximum 0.18), and across thirteen fully-adjudicated live sessions no attack was ever accepted. A full three-lane bona fide session reached ACCEPT with optical 0.75, typing 1.00 and acoustic 0.93 (joint 0.89). Ablation confirms that removing either real lane withdraws 100 % of acceptances, while a spatial attack that defeats the global optical score (0.95) is still exposed by a spatial score of −0.02. PRAESENS requires no additional hardware, no training data and no cloud service, and its per-verification operating cost is effectively nil.

Keywords: liveness detection, deepfake, video injection attack, challenge–response, m-sequence, presentation attack detection, sensor fusion, keystroke dynamics, acoustic sensing.
